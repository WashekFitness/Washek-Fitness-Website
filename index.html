Four ways to train, one system underneath.

Pick the style that fits how you want to train. The programming underneath — your goals, your equipment, your limits — carries over no matter which one you choose.

Calisthenics

Bodyweight, done in order

Progressions from your first pull-up to one-arm work, sequenced around the equipment you actually have.

Weighted calisthenics

Load added on purpose

Vests, belts, and bands added to bodyweight movements once you outgrow them — without losing the skill work underneath.

Weight lifting

Barbell and dumbbell

Programming built around your gym's equipment and your experience level, from your first cycle to your tenth.

Hybrid

Both, in one program

Calisthenics and weights in the same week, when neither one alone fits how you want to train.

It asks about you before it writes anything.

Every program starts from the same five questions — the answers are what actually shape the plan.

  • Equipment

    From a full gym to a resistance band on the living room floor.

  • Injuries and limitations

    Movements are chosen — or built around — what your body can actually do right now.

  • Gender

    Goals and recommendations are set with this in mind, not treated as one default body.

  • Goals

    Strength, size, fat loss, or a specific skill — the plan is written toward one clear target.

  • Other constraints

    How many days you can train, how long each session can run, and what your week actually looks like.
